ABM Custom Paintings — Discovery Questions
Prepared by: Touhidur Rahman Adil  ·  CX & Operations Consultant  ·  Based on 2022–2024 Refund Data
These 20 questions are based on a full 3-year analysis of ABM's refund data (2022–2024). Each question is backed by specific data evidence and links directly to a financial impact. The goal is to understand current processes so we can identify the fastest path from 7% to 4% refund rate.
📌
1. Checkout & System
Fastest fixes available
1
$85,717 over 3 years — 100% preventable
When a discount code fails to apply at checkout, is the customer automatically notified? Or do they have to notice it themselves and contact support?
art10, EASTER24, BF24, SUMMER24 — the same codes failing year after year from 2022 to 2024. This is a system issue, not a human error. One checkout fix eliminates this entirely.
2
$616,702 — largest single category
After placing an order, does the customer see a confirmation page showing the painting, size, frame, and total price together before payment is finalized?
"Ordered by mistake" / "Wrong CC" / "Changed my mind" — these 3 reasons account for 25–30% of the Other category. A strong order confirmation page reduces these significantly.
3
Est. $40,000–$60,000 per year
At checkout, can the customer see an estimated import duty or VAT amount for their country before completing the purchase?
Customers in the USA, UK, and EU are being shocked by customs bills at delivery. Adding one estimated duty line at checkout would eliminate most of these refunds.
4
Duplicate orders — consistent pattern
If a customer orders the same painting twice, does the checkout system show any warning or alert before the second order is placed?
Multiple "duplicate order ABM-XXXXX" cases found across 2023 and 2024. A simple real-time alert at checkout would stop this entirely.
📝
2. Sample Approval
$366,548 lost over 3 years
5
Most urgent — $366,548 over 3 years
Before production begins, does the system automatically block the order if there is no confirmed customer approval on file? Or is this checked manually by the agent?
$366,548 in Never Approved Sample refunds over 3 years. Without a system block, production can start even if the agent forgets to check approval.
6
~40–50% of all NAS refunds
If production modifies an approved sample — changing a color, size, or detail — is the customer asked to re-approve before production continues?
The data repeatedly shows "did not approve MODIFIED samples." The original approval is being treated as permanent, even after changes are made.
7
$66,595 in avoidable NAS — GREEN supplier
What process did the GREEN supplier follow in 2022? Their Never Approved Samples refunds were $0 that entire year — then jumped to $33,000+ in 2023.
A supplier that was perfect for a full year and then broke down is a process change, not a quality change. That changed process needs to be identified and restored.
8
BLUE supplier — 66% improvement
Has the BLUE supplier made any changes to their QC or approval process between 2022 and 2024? How did they reduce NAS from $85,000 to $28,000?
BLUE's improvement is the most important undocumented success in this dataset. Sharing their process with GREEN and YELLOW could save an additional $40,000 per year.
📷
3. Quality & Photography
$157,946 over 3 years — +204%
9
Fastest growing category — photography problem
Are the product photos on the website professionally color-calibrated to match the actual painting? How close is the color match between the website photo and the real product?
The #1 complaint across all 3 years: "colors not as vibrant as the website." This is a photography problem, not a production problem. Fixing photos saves $30,000–$40,000 per year.
10
Pre-ship approval opportunity
Before a painting is packaged and shipped, is a QC photo taken and sent to the customer for final approval?
If customers approve a final QC photo before shipping, "not what I expected" refunds would almost disappear — the customer has already confirmed what they are receiving.
11
YELLOW supplier — quality complaints not tracked
Is quality complaint data tracked separately by supplier? Which supplier generates the most quality-related refunds?
Analysis shows YELLOW supplier appearing most frequently in quality complaint entries across all 3 years. However, this is currently not tracked or visible in the data.
📦
4. Packaging & DHL
$180,597 over 3 years
12
DHL claims being missed
When a painting arrives damaged, is a DHL claim filed immediately for every order? Is there a written process defining who files it and within what timeframe?
When DHL damages a product during delivery, they are liable and must reimburse the business. Data suggests claims are being filed for some orders but not others — money is being left on the table.
13
Frame chips — most common damage type
For framed paintings, is corner protection or double-boxing used during packaging?
Frame chips and cracks are the most common damage type across all 3 years. This is a packaging failure, not a product defect. A packaging upgrade would directly reduce this category.
⚠
5. Chargeback & Legal Risk
$81,972 + Trustpilot ToS violation
14
$81,972 — winnable cases being lost
When a chargeback dispute arrives, is an evidence package submitted within 24 hours? Does the team have the order confirmation, sample approval email, delivery tracking, and delivery photo ready?
Multiple cases show delivered and confirmed orders still losing chargebacks. Evidence is not being submitted within the payment processor's dispute window.
15
Legal risk — must stop immediately
Is it current practice to offer customers a discount in exchange for changing a negative Trustpilot review? Does management know this is happening?
Refund records contain: "agreed to keep for 25% discount and will change 1-star review." This violates Trustpilot's Terms of Service and may breach consumer protection law in some markets.
📅
6. Seasonal Patterns & Operations
Recurring patterns every year
16
March — worst month every year, growing
Is any specific preparation done for March each year? What is the team's understanding of why March consistently has the highest refunds?
March 2022: $43,891 → March 2023: $49,618 → March 2024: $66,202. Growing every year with no pre-planned intervention in place.
17
November 2023 = $81,333 — all-time record
Before Black Friday and November campaigns, are all discount codes tested? Are suppliers briefed in advance about expected order volume increases?
November 2023 was the highest single month in the entire 3-year dataset. Three problems hit simultaneously: BF discount code failures, high volume, and supplier capacity strain.
18
Delay compensation — no written policy
When an order is delayed, what percentage discount are agents authorized to give? Is this written down anywhere as a formal policy?
Data shows some agents giving 10% and others giving 30% for the same type of delay. Without a written policy and cap, this cannot be controlled or predicted.
19
Weekly tracking — early warning needed
Is refund data reviewed on a weekly basis by anyone on the team? If a category spikes, how quickly does management find out?
November 2023 reached $81,333 with no early warning. A weekly dashboard would have flagged the spike in the first week — not at month-end when damage is already done.
20
August–September — safest window every year
August and September consistently have the lowest refunds every year. Is this period used for any team training, process improvements, or supplier reviews?
This is the ideal window for supplier audits, packaging upgrades, and system fixes — when volume is low and the team has capacity to focus on improvements.
